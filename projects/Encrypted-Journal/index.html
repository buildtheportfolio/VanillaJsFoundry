    How it's encrypted

    1. Key derivation. Your passphrase goes through PBKDF2 (HMAC-SHA-256, 600,000 iterations) with a random 16-byte salt to produce a 256-bit AES key.
    2. Encryption. The whole journal is sealed with AES-GCM using a brand new random 12-byte IV on every save. GCM also authenticates the data, so a wrong passphrase or tampering fails cleanly.
    3. Storage. Only a versioned envelope of salt, IV and ciphertext (base64) is saved in localStorage. Your passphrase and plaintext are never stored.
    4. In memory. The key is a non-extractable CryptoKey. Locking, auto-lock and closing the tab drop it along with decrypted entries.
    5. Limits. Anyone with your device and unlimited guesses can attack a weak passphrase offline, and malicious browser extensions or scripts on a compromised device can read what you type. Choose a long passphrase and keep a backup.
